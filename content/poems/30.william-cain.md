<p class="stanza"><span class="line">When William Cain came back to Centreville</span>
<span class="line">The first time since the day he left his people</span>
<span class="line">He found that storms had halved the Baptist steeple</span>
<span class="line">And a spring flood had carried off the mill</span></p>
<p class="stanza"><span class="line">And though he found a few old sweethearts still,</span>
<span class="line">Each apple cheek he once had pressed his lip in</span>
<span class="line">Was puckered like a sour Winter Pippin</span>
<span class="line">In which the worms of time had worked their will.</span></p>
<p class="stanza"><span class="line">But sadder somehow in these meetings was</span>
<span class="line">The sudden thought that took him unaware:</span>
<span class="line">How high-arched feet that danced upon the grass</span>
<span class="line">Or raced along the roadways brown and bare</span>
<span class="line">Had sagged beneath the growing weight of care</span>
<span class="line">To plod flat-footed like a spavined mare.</span></p>
